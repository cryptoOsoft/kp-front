The measure

«Usable at 3am – after six months without practice»

In a militia fire brigade nobody sits at the screen every day. So the app has to be built for someone half asleep to get right: everything that matters visible at once, sensible defaults instead of settings, and every action can be undone.

From the call to the report

One incident, one app.

This is what a night with KP Front looks like – the map, the teams and the documentation stay consistent and traceable for everyone involved.

03:01

Call

The call comes in.

Take over an alert from your paging system (e. g. Divera or FireHub) with one tap – or start by hand from an address, a building or the map. The location and the building data are there before the first appliance rolls.

03:07

Situation

The first situation picture is up.

FKS tactical symbols, sectors, hose lines and notes on the digital situation map. Hydrants, utility lines and vehicle positions sit underneath as layers.

03:12

Teams

Team 1 goes in.

Cylinder pressure, turnaround time and contact clock run per team. The app speaks up before it gets tight – even when nobody happens to be watching the clock.

03:18

Plan

The building plan becomes a whiteboard.

Open the pre-incident plan, draw on it, place teams, measure to scale – with the same tools as on the map.

03:40

Log

Everything is in the log.

Type it, or simply speak it – when a transcription service is configured, voice memos can be turned into text; otherwise they stay as audio in the log. The log is only ever added to, never rewritten – corrections use new entries, not an eraser.

04:30

Shift plan

Relief is planned, not guessed.

If it turns into a long night, the shift plan holds, per person, from when to when they are available and when they are rostered. The coverage line shows where there are too few people – before the gap is there. Attendance is untouched by it: planning happens here, ticking off happens there.

05:55

Report

By the time you head back, the report is nearly done.

Situation, crew, material and log flow straight into the incident report. Nobody has to reconstruct the night from memory afterwards.

Afterwards

Replay

The night runs once more.

For the debrief, the replay plays the whole incident back: situation, teams and log exactly as they stood at any given minute. It skips the long waits – four hours become a few minutes.

Scope

What is in it.

Grown at real incidents and exercises, not from a feature list. Everything that comes from outside – paging, GPS, Microsoft sign-in – is optional: KP Front runs perfectly well without any of those connections.

Map & plan

  • Tactical symbols to FKS conventions, with attributes and labelling
  • Sectors, cordons, areas and freehand drawing
  • Hose lines with FKS annotations, each assigned to a team
  • Measuring distance and area, to scale on the plan as well
  • Layers: hydrants, utility cadastre, cantonal map services
  • Hazardous materials reference: UN numbers, substance information and guidance on isolation and protective distances, available directly during the incident
  • Weather and wind information to support the situation assessment, plus the MeteoSwiss precipitation radar as a layer
  • Building plans as a whiteboard and a stack of floors
  • Board with FKS pages: free sketch, First command and handbook sheets – printed in the report like the original
  • Building info: facts from the building register – gas, heating oil, solar PV – and the immediate measures for the object
  • Georeferencing: link a plan to the map through shared landmarks – one object, visible on both surfaces, with an honest read-out of the accuracy. The station has its plans aligned ahead of time and approves them
  • Undo/redo on every surface – documented events remain traceable

Crew & resources

  • SCBA monitoring: pressure, turnaround time, contact clock, alarms
  • SCBA emergency: a team in trouble turns red at the top of every device at once – with an emergency clock and the safety team
  • Teams with or without breathing apparatus: crew, assignment and team leader – on the map, on the plan and in the stack of floors, each with its own team colour, the same everywhere
  • Attendance with arrival times and ranks
  • On the way: who has confirmed the Divera alert, right above attendance
  • Shift plan: plan availability and shifts per person, see coverage across the incident and print command forms
  • Share location: whoever opens the link appears as a dot on the situation map – voluntary, and only for this incident
  • Share the incident by link; access is clearly indicated and can be revoked at any time
  • Material with stock and consumption
  • Checklists for command support and incident command
  • Incident commander role: keep attendance, resources, checklists and the report – read the map and the teams without moving anything

Documentation

  • A log that is only added to and never rewritten
  • Voice memos with transcription, photos, reminders
  • Replay: play the incident back minute by minute, waits skipped – for the debrief
  • Ready-made incident report as a PDF, including crew, material, times, log, sketch, photos and SCBA monitoring
  • Debrief sheet for the after-action review: key figures and a timeline on a page of its own at the end of the report
  • Photos as attachments to the report
  • Completion check: before closing, KP Front shows which information or clarifications are still missing from the report
  • Partner organisations with their remarks in the report
  • A capture sheet by QR code for everyone who has something to contribute
  • Alert and incident times from what actually happened, not from memory

Running it

  • Offline-capable as an installable app – the readiness check shows before an incident whether the required data is on the device
  • Several connected devices work on the same state – after a connection interruption, changes are synchronised again
  • Roles: edit, or read along only
  • Day/night mode, symbol size, large touch targets for the tablet
  • Can take over alerts from Divera or FireHub; further alerting systems can be connected through the open interface optional
  • Vehicle positions by GPS optional
  • Sign in with Microsoft to an existing account, alongside the PIN optional
  • Object visits without an incident, offline – checklist, photos and report optional
  • Your own administration area per fire brigade: crew, vehicles, checklists, map layers and templates

Questions & answers

The questions we get most.

Why switch?

Everyone sees the same situation at the same time – in the command vehicle, on the tablet and with the incident commander. No message is lost on a scrap of paper. The SCBA monitoring speaks up by itself before it gets tight. Less radio traffic for questions that are already answered on the situation picture – and the report is nearly done as you head back, instead of the weekend after. KP Front helps the incident commander keep the situation free of contradictions and make the odd one out visible: a team out of radio contact, an appliance that is nowhere, a check-in that never came.

Where is sensitive incident data stored?

Every fire brigade runs KP Front itself (self-hosted). Incident data – situation map, SCBA monitoring, log and building plans – stays on its own installation and its own devices: for example a mini-PC at the station, its own server or a cloud provider it chooses. There is no central server, and nothing is sent automatically to a vendor or to us. Map, address and weather services (swisstopo among them by default) receive only the location being requested; other external services run only when the brigade sets them up itself.

How does KP Front differ from commercial systems?

KP Front is open source (AGPL-3.0): no licence fees, no subscription, no per-device fee. Every fire brigade runs its own installation and so keeps control of its data. What remains are its own costs for operation, infrastructure and any external support. Development comes straight from the practice of Feuerwehr Oberwil BL; bug reports, ideas and contributions from the open-source community are welcome.

What does it take to run?

A small mini-PC or server at the station is enough. Setup, updates and backups require someone with Docker and command-line experience, whether in the brigade, the municipality or an IT support provider. The guide and administration area lead through the setup. We are happy to answer general questions; data and operation remain the responsibility of each fire brigade.

Is support available for installation and operation?

Yes. General questions about installation and use can be answered free of charge. KP Front can be operated on an organisation’s own infrastructure or with a hosting or cloud provider of its choice. Support with installation, configuration, updates or operation is also available by individual agreement. More extensive support may be subject to a fee.

Does KP Front work without an internet connection?

Yes. As an installed app, KP Front runs on the device: map, plans, teams and log stay usable even when the network drops. The app loads the maps and plans around an incident by itself, shortly after it is opened – and the readiness check shows before an incident whether the required data is on the device. After an interruption, changes are synchronised again as soon as a connection is available; whatever was recorded in the meantime is kept.

Can we adapt it to how we work?

Yes. Every fire brigade has its own administration area: crew, vehicles, doctrine and templates are all set there. Checklists, building plans and map layers come from your own station data and are loaded with the command-line tools that ship with it. And because the whole code is on GitHub , anything else can be built – by you or to your order.

Does KP Front only work in Switzerland?

No. As open-source software, KP Front is freely available and can be adapted to local conditions anywhere in the world. Out of the box it is preconfigured for Switzerland – with swisstopo maps, tactical symbols to FKS conventions and the FKS templates for incident command. Outside Switzerland other base maps can be configured; address search currently supports only the swisstopo interface and needs adapting there.

Which languages are available for KP Front?

The KP Front user interface is currently available in German. As an open-source application, KP Front can be extended to further languages. This requires a complete translation of the firefighting terminology and a professional review.

How do we start?

With the demo, no sign-up – Musterdorf is already on fire, and all visitors share the same incident (it restarts at midnight and at noon). Then try it at an exercise. If it fits, the setup guide leads to your own installation.
